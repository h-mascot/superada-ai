Updated: 2026-07-01

The correction

Weekly Claw should not be built around OpenClaw anymore.

OpenClaw is the origin story and may remain part of the transition period because the show currently lives in/around the OpenClaw Discord, but the new Weekly Claw should not be constrained by OpenClaw’s roadmap, community updates, or internal news cycle.

The future version should go wherever the interesting AI/building/devtools/agent news is happening.

If OpenClaw has genuinely valuable news, we talk about OpenClaw. If not, we do not force it into the episode.

Vision

Weekly Claw becomes a broader AI builder media brand for people who want to understand what is actually happening at the edge of AI, agents, devtools, startups, workflows, and internet-native building.

It should feel closer to a founder/operator/builder show than a project community update.

The show should become a place where people come for:

  • sharp takes on what matters in AI and devtools
  • demos and workflows from builders actually using the tools
  • live reactions to important launches and debates
  • conversations with people building interesting things
  • practical taste on where software and agentic work are going

The ambition is not “OpenClaw weekly updates.”

The ambition is: a media brand with its own gravity.

One-line positioning

Weekly Claw is a live builder show about AI, agents, devtools, startups, and the weird edge of software — following the news and builders wherever the interesting stuff is happening.

Why pivot

The current Weekly Claw format has useful infrastructure: a name, cadence, Discord audience, hosts, website, show notes, deck format, and a community habit.

But if it stays only an OpenClaw show, the ceiling is too low.

The better play is to turn it into a broader attention engine:

  • grows the Weekly Claw brand
  • grows the hosts’ personal brands
  • creates distribution for projects the team is building
  • keeps optional upside for OpenClaw when OpenClaw has real news
  • allows the show to follow the actual market instead of forcing a narrow agenda

The Theo/T3 model is the reference: build audience and trust around people, then that attention compounds into product distribution.

Strategic bet

The core bet is that personal brands + live builder media + real distribution beats project-only community programming.

Weekly Claw should become a platform, not a support show.

That means:

  • personality matters
  • speed matters
  • strong opinions matter
  • clips matter
  • recurring faces matter
  • distribution matters as much as the live episode
  • the show should be allowed to outgrow its original container

Relationship to OpenClaw

OpenClaw should be treated as:

  • the origin story
  • a possible recurring topic
  • a transition home while the show still runs through Discord
  • one source of news when there is something genuinely interesting

OpenClaw should not be treated as:

  • the show’s boundary
  • the mandatory main topic every week
  • the only audience
  • the reason the brand exists long term

Possible transition language:

Weekly Claw started in the OpenClaw community, but the next version is broader. We’ll still cover OpenClaw when there is real news, but the show is now about the wider world of AI builders, agents, devtools, and where software is going.

Reference examples: X-first / live internet shows

MTS — @MTSlive

URL: https://x.com/mtslive?s=21

Observed positioning from X profile: “Chronicling the singularity.” The account is X-native, high-frequency, and built around tracking the AI moment in public.

What Weekly Claw can learn:

  • a simple big-frame premise can carry the brand
  • X-native distribution can be the primary surface, not an afterthought
  • the account can be a running editorial feed, not only a place to post episode links
  • strong curation and framing can build audience even before YouTube depth

TBPN — @tbpn

URL: https://x.com/tbpn?s=21

Observed positioning from X profile: “Technology’s daily show.” Hosted by John Coogan and Jordi Hays. Streaming live 11a–2p PT every weekday, with a newsletter at TBPN.com.

What Weekly Claw can learn:

  • clear show identity: “technology’s daily show” is simple and legible
  • hosts are central to the brand, not hidden behind it
  • live programming plus clips/newsletter creates a daily media loop
  • the show can be both personality-led and news-led
  • X can be the social broadcast layer while the website/newsletter captures durable audience

Channel strategy

X — primary growth channel

X should be the first-class channel, not just a place to dump links.

Use X for:

  • live clips
  • short takes
  • episode hooks
  • quote posts on breaking AI/devtools news
  • founder/operator opinions
  • polls
  • guest clips
  • “what just happened?” explainers
  • host account amplification

If the show is going to grow as a media brand, X is the most important early surface.

YouTube — depth and evergreen

YouTube should become the long-form archive and discovery engine.

Use YouTube for:

  • full episodes
  • edited highlight cuts
  • Shorts
  • topical explainers
  • guest segments
  • recurring series

Do not wait for perfect production. Start with watchable, consistent, clipped content.

Discord — transition/community layer

Discord can remain the live/community home during the transition, especially because the current show already has that habit.

But Discord should not define the show’s long-term identity.

Use Discord for:

  • live chat
  • community questions
  • after-show discussion
  • early fans
  • guest/community demos

Eventually, the show can move away from Discord if the audience and format demand it.

Website — canonical archive

weeklyclaw.ai should remain the durable hub.

Use it for:

  • episode pages
  • links and sources
  • guest/project links
  • embeds of X/YouTube content
  • written recaps
  • sponsor/partner info later
  • audience capture if newsletter becomes useful

Newsletter — later, not first

A newsletter can work later, especially if the show becomes a weekly/daily briefing brand. But it should not become an extra obligation before the X/YouTube loop works.

Content types

Flagship live episode

The main show. Could be weekly at first. Designed for clips and distribution, not just live attendance.

What happened this week

Not generic AI news. Curated: what actually matters for AI builders, agents, devtools, startups, and workflows.

Hot takes / debate segment

The part that travels on X. Hosts disagree, make predictions, and say the thing plainly.

Builder demo

Someone shows a workflow, tool, agent setup, product, or weird automation.

Tool fight

Cursor vs Claude Code vs OpenClaw vs Codex vs local models vs whatever is hot. These comparisons are useful and clip-friendly.

Agent/workflow failure segment

A funny/honest segment about what broke, what agents messed up, what took too long, or what a tool failed at.

Guest builder

Bring in people building in the AI/devtools/agent ecosystem.

Project leverage segment

Each host can occasionally show what they are building, but it should be framed as useful/interesting content, not self-promotion.

Breaking-news live reaction

When a big model/tool/company/news event drops, Weekly Claw can do fast X Spaces/live reactions or short emergency episodes.

Editorial rule

The show follows the excitement.

If OpenClaw is the excitement that week, OpenClaw is in the show.

If the excitement is Cursor, Anthropic, OpenAI, local models, Devin, Claude Code, Perplexity, Lovable, TBPN-style tech news, AI Twitter drama, or a weird builder workflow, then that is where the show goes.

No forced OpenClaw slot.

Format options

Option A — Weekly flagship

One strong weekly show, heavily clipped.

Best for starting.

Option B — X-first recurring live show

Shorter, more frequent X-native live conversations, with YouTube as archive.

Best if the team wants to imitate the live internet media feel more directly.

Option C — Hybrid

Weekly full show plus occasional X live reactions when something big happens.

Recommended starting point.

Goals

First 30 days

  • agree the pivot and stop describing the show as OpenClaw-only
  • define the first 4 episodes under the broader premise
  • publish every episode with clips
  • post from the Weekly Claw account and host accounts
  • test X-native clips/takes against YouTube uploads
  • include OpenClaw only when there is real news

First 90 days

  • establish recurring segments
  • identify the strongest host dynamics
  • bring in the first external guests
  • grow X as the main discovery channel
  • make YouTube the full-episode/highlight archive
  • prove at least one content format can travel beyond the existing OpenClaw audience

Metrics

Track:

  • X follower growth
  • average clip views
  • reposts/quote posts from builders
  • YouTube subscribers
  • YouTube watch time
  • Discord participation during transition
  • guest quality/inbound interest
  • traffic or signups to projects mentioned naturally through the show
  • growth of host personal accounts

The key question: is the show creating attention that compounds?

Team and roles

Direction / executive producer

Owns the taste, positioning, and long-term ambition.

Showrunner

Owns weekly cadence, rundown, guest coordination, and making sure the show happens.

Hosts

Recurring faces. The hosts are part of the product. Audience should know who they are and why to follow them.

Clips/distribution owner

Probably the most important operational role. Without clips, the show does not grow.

Research/rundown owner

Finds the week’s real stories and filters out generic AI noise.

Website/archive owner

Keeps weeklyclaw.ai useful and makes every episode page a durable artifact.

Community/live owner

Manages Discord/live questions during the transition.

Later roles

  • editor
  • producer
  • guest booker
  • designer/motion person
  • newsletter operator
  • sponsor/partnership owner

Initial team ask

The first discussion should not be about every detail.

The first discussion should answer:

  1. Do we agree Weekly Claw is no longer built around OpenClaw?
  2. Do we agree OpenClaw becomes an occasional topic, not the center?
  3. Do we want X to become the primary growth surface?
  4. Do we want YouTube to become the long-form archive?
  5. Who owns the showrunner/distribution roles for the next 4 episodes?
  6. What are the first 4 broader episodes we should test?

Company lens: what kind of business Tilt is

Public profile + first-call context. Correction: the relevant Tilt appears to be the UK live-shopping / social-commerce company, not the US fintech formerly called Empower. That changes the headwinds materially.

Public profile

Scale: venture-backed, high-growth team

Public company pages describe Tilt as a 21–100 person London/Kings Cross live-shopping company. Other public datasets estimate about 66 employees, so use a range rather than over-claiming LinkedIn precision.

  • Large enough for manager-consistency problems to show up.
  • Small enough that a 2–4 week workflow pilot can still move quickly.
  • Likely founder-led, urgency-heavy, and still shaping operating systems.
Public positioning

Live commerce, not generic ecommerce

Tilt is building a live-shopping marketplace where sellers perform, build community, and transact in real time. Public coverage says it passed 500k users in its first year and raised a £13.7m / $18m Series A led by Balderton.

  • The product sits at the intersection of marketplace, creator economy, ecommerce, and consumer app growth.
  • Performance pressure will be tied to speed, seller success, trust, growth, and product execution.
  • The ex-Revolut founder background explains the high bar / high velocity operating style.

Tilt’s Snap feature creates product listings from live video in under a second. Public reporting claims 94.8% product identification accuracy and a 47% increase in sell-through / sales efficiency versus 32% manually.

Implication

They need performance as scale infrastructure

At Tilt’s stage, the goal is not nicer reviews. It is a system that helps managers keep a high bar, capture better evidence, coach faster, and scale a founder-quality operating culture without turning the company into review-bureaucracy soup. Horrible soup. No one wants that soup.

Headwinds Tilt is probably facing

These are the business pressures that make Performance Management a strategic wedge rather than an HR side quest.

Marketplace cold-start and liquidity

Live commerce only works if supply, demand, content quality, and transaction trust compound together. Teams can be busy without moving liquidity.

Performance implication: performance signals should connect work to marketplace outcomes: seller activation, buyer repeat, stream quality, category growth, conversion, retention.

Creator / seller operations complexity

Sellers are both operators and performers. Tilt’s own Snap narrative is about removing admin so sellers can focus on the audience.

Performance implication: managers need to evaluate whether teams reduce friction and increase seller throughput, not just ship internal tasks.

Consumer growth efficiency

Paid/social/community acquisition can get expensive fast. Performance and growth teams need tight loops between experiments, insight, spend, and product changes.

Performance implication: reviews should reward learning velocity, commercial judgment, and measurable compounding loops, not random heroics.

Trust, safety, and brand risk

Live marketplaces deal with authenticity, disputes, refunds, seller behavior, buyer trust, content moderation, and potentially counterfeit or restricted goods risk.

Performance implication: performance rubrics should include judgment, escalation quality, audit hygiene, and customer/seller trust behaviors.

AI-native ambition vs middle-layer adoption

Leadership is bought in, but middle-layer adoption varies. Engineers fear losing craft. Training has been clunky.

Performance implication: evaluate better outcomes and judgment, not raw Claude usage. Otherwise they create resistance dressed as metrics.

Small leverage teams, many managers

People, Performance, and AI Enablement appear small relative to company ambition. Manual coaching and review clean-up will not scale.

Performance implication: agents should multiply Liz/Gena/Ben’s operating leverage without becoming black-box decision-makers.

Performance agents I’d recommend

Start with the safest, highest-ROI wedge, then expand into a portfolio. The order matters.

1 · Start here

Performance Signal Agent

Defines and gathers approved performance signals from goals, projects, feedback, context repo, manager notes, and selected work artifacts.

  • Outputs source-backed signal brief.
  • Flags sparse / stale / one-sided evidence.
  • Separates outcome evidence from activity evidence.
2

Review Prep Agent

Turns approved evidence into manager-ready review prep without assigning ratings.

  • Drafts review talking points.
  • Maps evidence to rubric.
  • Highlights unsupported claims.
  • Suggests questions for missing context.
3

Calibration Evidence Agent

Prepares a packet for People/performance calibration so managers arrive with comparable evidence.

  • Compares rationale quality, not people.
  • Flags rating/rubric mismatch.
  • Surfaces outliers for discussion.
4

Manager Coach Agent

Helps managers deliver feedback clearly and humanely, especially where AI/craft anxiety exists.

  • SBI-style feedback rehearsal.
  • Difficult conversation prep.
  • Craft-preserving AI adoption language.
5

Promotion Transparency Agent

Creates promotion readiness/rationale packs that show what evidence supports the case and what gaps remain.

  • Not a promotion decider.
  • Useful for “why are people promoted?”
  • Improves fairness perception if governed well.
6

AI-Native Work Adoption Agent

Helps teams identify good AI usage patterns and learning needs without turning raw Claude usage into a score.

  • Evaluates workflow outcomes, not usage vanity metrics.
  • Finds coaching opportunities.
  • Protects engineer craft concerns.
7

Trust & Marketplace Quality Agent

For operations, seller success, trust/safety, and marketplace teams, links performance expectations to seller outcomes, escalation quality, audit hygiene, and customer trust.

  • Useful for ops, seller success, trust/safety, and category teams.
  • Turns “good judgment” into observable behaviors.
  • Keeps sensitive seller/customer data boundaries explicit.
8

Operating Capacity Agent

Looks across teams for bottlenecks, review debt, unclear ownership, and manager overload.

  • Helps Liz/Gena see where managers need support.
  • Supports remote-first scaling.
  • Good phase-two executive dashboard.
Recommendation

Pilot bundle

Do not pitch eight agents at once. Pitch a three-agent MVP bundle: Performance Signal Agent + Review Prep Agent + Calibration Evidence Agent. Mention the others as expansion paths after proof of value.

Inferred objective

Use Performance Management as a high-leverage proof point for AI-native work: clearer performance signals, lower admin burden, better promotion transparency, and a workflow managers trust.

Their asks

  • Implicit: fit Claude/Cowork/context repo.

Recommended MVP

Make managers more evidenced and consistent. Do not make AI the judge.

Recommended

Performance Signal & Review Calibration Agent

An agentic workflow that assembles source-backed evidence, maps it to the rubric, flags weak claims/missing signals, reduces manager prep time, and creates calibration packets for People.

  • Manager pre-review brief
  • Evidence map against rubric
  • Missing-signal checklist
  • Unsupported-claim flags
  • Coaching prompts for feedback delivery
  • Calibration packet + audit trail
Do not do

AI performance decisioning

Phase one should explicitly exclude high-risk decision automation.

  • No automated ratings
  • No promotion/comp/PIP/termination recommendations
  • No opaque employee scoring
  • No raw Claude-usage-as-performance metric
  • No private DM surveillance

Use this language

The words matter because people-data + AI can get creepy fast.

Opener

“Your agenda already points to Performance Management as the high-leverage area. Our goal today should be to leave with a concrete MVP workflow, not a generic AI transformation plan. Since the agenda is explicitly about performance signals and admin burden, I’d suggest we test review prep and calibration as the first wedge — manager judgment with better evidence, not AI deciding performance.”

Close

“If we agree this is the right wedge, the next step should be a one-page pilot charter: MVP workflow, cohort, data in/out, outputs, success metrics, controls, timeline, and the approval path. Then you can decide quickly whether this is worth funding as a bounded pilot.”

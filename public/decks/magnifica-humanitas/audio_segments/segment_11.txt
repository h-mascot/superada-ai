== SEGMENT 11: CONCLUSION — THE WORD BECAME FLESH ==

The conclusion of the encyclical proposes a sober yet demanding program of Christian life. The Pope begins by returning to the mystery of the Incarnation — the Word who became flesh and dwelt among us.

He contrasts the promises of transhumanism, which seek an enhanced and almost disembodied humanity, with the mystery of the Son of God entering into our human condition. The living God descends into our history in order to free us from all forms of slavery. He takes upon himself our weakness and transforms it into a setting for salvation.

Citing the French theologian Pierre de Bérulle: "According to the teaching of our faith, we have and adore, in our mysteries, a God who is born in a manger, a God who lives and travels in Judea, a God who dies on the cross, a dead God who lies in the tomb. O wonder… man is God and this God-Man passes through all those stages, endures all those states and ennobles them, sanctifies them, deifies them in himself!"

What saves humanity, the Pope insists, is the divine love that descends into the most fragile point of our history and renews it from within.

He then makes a beautiful affirmation: "No computational system, however sophisticated, can create a heart that gives itself, or a conscience that discerns good from evil. Even when machines excel in efficiency, a human face that asks to be gazed upon remains the center of our history."

He speaks of the mystery of recapitulation — the certainty that the Father has decreed to bring all things back to Christ, the one Head. In this plan, nothing will be lost that is authentically human. Everything will be purified and reunited in the One, who gathers every fragment of life, every tear, and every authentically human achievement, rescuing them from nothingness and delivering them, redeemed, to the Father.

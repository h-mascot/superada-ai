Concept by Henry Mascot · Nigeria · August 2026

The Transformation Collective

A private-sector institution built to find, back, connect, and develop Nigerians who can strengthen the country’s most important institutions across generations.

A 50-year institution producing measurable national outcomes every five years.
Horizon50 years
First results3 to 5 years
ModelThree generations
LoyaltyMission, not founder
The idea

Change needs a talent system that survives election cycles.

Nigeria has no shortage of capable people. It has a shortage of durable systems that find them early, develop them deliberately, and keep them connected long enough to improve institutions.

The Transformation Collective would work across public service, law, finance, technology, media, education, security, infrastructure, and company building. It would fund opportunity, create apprenticeship routes, provide serious mentorship, and build a lifelong network around a constitutional vision of a stronger Nigeria.

This is an early concept, not a launched organisation. The useful work now is to pressure-test the governance, selection, funding, safeguarding, and measurement model before romance turns into expensive nonsense.

Three pipelines

Do not wait twenty years for the first result.

AGES 30–50

Builders

Back credible people who already run companies, schools, courts, public programmes, media organisations, policy shops, or civic institutions. Results should show within three to five years.

AGES 20–35

Fellows

Combine education, apprenticeships, capital, international exposure, strategic career support, and a network that can open doors without becoming a patronage machine.

AGES 12–20

Scholars

Find exceptional young people across every state, class, religion, and ethnicity. Invest in education, health, character, service, and long-term mentorship.

Scale

One hundred people is a pilot. The institution needs redundancy.

100strategic leaders across consequential institutions
1,000senior operators who can execute and reproduce the model
10,000trained alumni and local builders across Nigeria

Regional pipelines reduce capture by any one city, ethnic bloc, donor, or political faction. A permanent endowment reduces dependence on government budgets and founder mood swings. Succession must be designed before the founder becomes irreplaceable.

50-year path

Build the institution in decades. Demand evidence every five years.

Years 0–10

Foundation

Charter, governance, endowment, safeguarding, selection, first cohorts.

Years 10–20

Formation

Education, apprenticeships, diaspora exposure, and local service.

Years 20–30

Institution entry

Alumni build and strengthen companies, schools, courts, media, and policy shops.

Years 30–40

Network effects

The network appoints, funds, mentors, and develops the next generation.

Years 40–50

Succession

Second-generation leadership makes the institution independent of its founders.

Failure modes

The hardest problem is avoiding a scholarship programme that becomes a private patronage network.

No loyalty oath to a person

Members serve a constitutional mission and public outcomes. Founder worship should disqualify the design.

Independent selection

Publish criteria, use regional panels, audit decisions, and protect the process from donor and political pressure.

Competing institutions

Place people across institutions and viewpoints. A network that requires ideological obedience will become brittle and dangerous.

Measured public outcomes

Track institutions improved, systems built, people served, and reforms that survive leadership changes.

Safeguarding before scale

Working with minors requires professional child protection, family consent, independent complaints, and strict limits on influence.

Succession from day one

Term limits, independent trustees, distributed funding, and published governance should stop the mission becoming private property.

Founding principle
Build people who are loyal to a constitutional vision of Nigeria, not loyal to Henry Mascot.
Precedents

No precedent matches the full model. Several prove useful parts.

Singapore Public Service Commission

A reference for deliberate talent selection, scholarships, and structured public-service careers. Its model is state-run, while the Collective is conceived as private-sector infrastructure.

Rhodes Scholarship

Questions to answer

The concept becomes credible when these have hard answers.

Who appoints and removes trustees, and how is political or donor capture detected?

What selection evidence predicts service, judgment, and institution-building rather than exam performance alone?

Which five-year national outcomes can be measured without rewarding vanity metrics?

How does the Collective support careers without directing members as a political bloc?

What endowment size, donor mix, and spending rule could survive fifty years?

What should the first three-year pilot prove before the model touches minors or expands nationally?

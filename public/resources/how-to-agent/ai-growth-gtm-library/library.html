03 / Put it to work

Practical playbooks

1. Find the narrow customer problem

Build the GTM context from customer evidence, then let agents reuse and challenge it.

  1. Pick one proposed buyer and one painful recurring job. Write the current workaround, cost of delay and why a buyer would switch.
  2. For an initial learning batch, observe 5–10 target buyers doing the job. Keep verbatim problems, objections, triggers and alternatives with source links.
  3. Create versioned files for the customer profile, evidence, positioning, alternatives and unresolved questions. Separate observed facts from hypotheses.
  4. Have an agent propose three narrower segments and the evidence against each. The project owner chooses the first wedge; revise after the next customer conversations.

What to measure

Repeated problem evidenceQualified conversationsWillingness to try/payObjections resolved

Proposed adaptation for the product; not an implemented workflow or a promised result. Suggested sample sizes are starting experiments, not industry benchmarks.

2. Build an evidence-led account-development desk

Use AI to improve relevance and research quality before increasing outbound volume.

  1. Create a small initial set of accounts matching the wedge. For each, retain the buying trigger, relevant person, source date and evidence of the problem.
  2. Resolve identity and verify every material enrichment claim against its source. Hold uncertain accounts instead of inventing facts.
  3. Draft a useful diagnosis, example or mini-audit specific to the account; have a human review claims and the proposed next step.
  4. When outreach is separately authorized, test messages in small comparable batches. Feed qualified replies, objections, meetings and wins/losses back into targeting and copy.

What to measure

Verified account precisionPositive qualified repliesQualified meetingsPipeline per hour and dollarResearch error rate

3. Run a follow-up and customer-learning loop

Put inbound, trials, renewals and objections into a maintained queue with owners and next actions.

  1. Define lifecycle stages and the evidence required to move an account between them. Keep one source of truth for conversations and commitments.
  2. Have agents summarize context, propose the next action and flag missing information. Use explicit approval rules and escalation for consequential replies.
  3. Set follow-up timers and inspect stalled conversations. Record why messages or actions were edited, accepted or rejected.
  4. Review outcomes weekly, adjust one failure pattern, then measure whether the changed playbook helped.

What to measure

Time to relevant responseNext-action coverageTrial-to-value conversionStalled-account recoveryHuman review time

4. Make product value visible quickly

Treat activation, willingness to pay and repeat use as separate questions.

  1. Choose a first-session action that produces a useful outcome for the chosen buyer. Instrument completion and time to value.
  2. Watch real new users reach or miss that outcome. Fix the largest friction before adding acquisition spend.
  3. Test pricing or packaging on comparable new-user cohorts with a named owner and a defined decision rule; preserve existing commitments.
  4. Track retained use, expansion and cost to serve alongside checkout conversion. Use internal dogfooding for diagnosis, then validate with external target users.

What to measure

5. Turn one useful demonstration into distribution

Create assets from real work and learn which channels produce qualified demand.

  1. Record one concrete customer problem and product demonstration with checkable claims.
  2. Use AI to propose channel-specific clips, posts, articles and follow-up material. Review factual claims and preserve the speaker’s intended meaning.
  3. Test a small set of relevant communities or niche creators; personally help early partners experience the product.
  4. Connect each asset to relevant visits, qualified signups and retained use. Repeat the message/channel combinations that work; stop scaling weak ones.

What to measure

Qualified visitsActivated signups by sourceCreator/cohort retentionCost per retained account

6. Make experimentation a weekly operating rhythm

Use compute to investigate and improve decisions, not merely generate more content.

  1. Keep an experiment ledger: hypothesis, segment, intervention, cost, primary metric, quality checks, decision date and owner.
  2. Ask agents to analyze customer language, raw campaign results and realistic buyer search prompts. Preserve receipts and contrary evidence.
  3. Ship one or two bounded changes at a time. Distinguish observed correlation from a controlled comparison.
  4. Every week, retain, revise or stop each experiment. Update the operating documents with findings and exact conditions, not universal slogans.

03 / Put it to work

Practical playbooks

1. Build a case an investor can verify

A large ambition is more credible when the customer wedge, product evidence and expansion path fit together.

  1. Write a plain-language memo: customer, painful job, product, why now, differentiated insight, evidence, business model, team and capital needed.
  2. Show a live customer workflow and the underlying evidence. Separate actual usage/revenue from pipeline, waitlists, forecasts and reported run rate.
  3. Build a bottom-up market view from reachable buyers and plausible spending; label assumptions and show the initial wedge and expansion route.
  4. Use repeated questioning to expose weak assumptions. Update the memo and evidence, rather than adding unsupported claims to the pitch.

What to measure

Clarity without narrationEvidence coverageUnresolved objectionsCustomer references

Proposed preparation process. A concise memo complements the format investors request; a no-deck approach is not universally suitable.

2. Prove demand, retention and AI economics

Show that customers keep getting value after the initial novelty and that delivering it can support a durable business.

  1. Define activation and retain cohort tables for actual target users. Keep paid accounts, active users, contracted ARR and annualized revenue distinct.
  2. Measure successful customer outcomes with the required quality level; include failed attempts, retries and human review.
  3. Record model/inference, infrastructure, support and human-service costs per workflow and account. Test the effect of heavier usage and changing model prices.
  4. Explain the source of durable differentiation: workflow integration, distribution, trust, proprietary access or feedback effects, with evidence for each.

What to measure

Account and revenue retentionCost per successful outcomeGross margin by cohortExpansionAcquisition payback

Use your product’s observed data. Model-provider retention, hypothetical margin examples and famous-company multiples are not the product benchmarks.

3. Size the raise around the next valuable milestone

Compare what different financing amounts allow you to prove, and what each costs in dilution and obligations.

  1. Define the next milestone that reduces a major business risk: retained paying demand, repeatable acquisition, enterprise deployment or another stage-appropriate proof.
  2. Model monthly operating cash with base and downside revenue, hiring, inference and infrastructure. Keep one-off financing and transaction costs in a separate schedule. Separate bookings from cash collection.
  3. A planning identity is: funding gap = expected net operating cash use through the milestone + contingency + one-off financing and transaction costs − deployable cash. Net operating cash use excludes the one-off costs added separately. Reconcile timing and committed funding before choosing an ask.
  4. Compare smaller, base and larger raises against speed, hiring execution, runway, ownership and future financing risk. Choose based on the operating plan, not peers’ headline rounds.

What to measure

Months to milestoneBase/downside cash runwayCapital per proof pointOwnership scenariosFinancing dependency

The formula is a planning identity, not a valuation or financing recommendation. Historical numerical heuristics are dated; actual terms and instruments require qualified transaction advice.

4. Select investors and manage a real process

Match the actual partner, stage, check size, ownership expectations and company needs.

  1. Build a research table of relevant partners using current public investment evidence. Record thesis, stage, geography, check/ownership assumptions, conflicts and why the partnership fits.
  2. Reference-check concrete help and behavior when a company struggles. Separate a fund’s marketing promise from founders’ experience.
  3. Prepare the core materials and practice before priority meetings. Run a bounded sequence with explicit owners, next steps and deadlines that allow real diligence.
  4. Track interest, diligence, written terms, executed documents and funds received as different states. Maintain honest alternatives; never invent competing offers.

What to measure

Qualified partner fitMeeting-to-next-step conversionTime in diligenceUnanswered diligence itemsCash received

Preparation only; no investors were contacted. Public portfolio evidence does not establish current mandate, willingness to invest or permission to share confidential data.

5. Use AI as a fundraising analyst and preparation partner

Reduce preparation work while keeping factual claims and confidential disclosure under founder control.

  1. Organize an evidence room: company narrative, cap table, financial model, cohorts, customer references, product/security material and an assumption register.
  2. Have AI draft a claim-to-source map and challenging investor questions. Verify every answer against the actual source; keep missing evidence visible.
  3. Draft individualized meeting briefs and follow-up answers from an approved evidence set. Check numbers, confidentiality and attachments before any authorized sharing.
  4. Use objections and diligence questions to improve the product case, model and memo. Track repeated concerns and whether the underlying evidence resolves them.

What to measure

Preparation timeClaim traceabilityAnswer turnaroundCorrection rateRepeated objections resolved

Our proposed AI-assisted workflow. Srinivas’s AI-assisted preparation and live-Q&A account is self-reported; the complete workflow here was not tested on the product.

6. Study large rounds without copying their advantages

Extract the investor logic and operating proof, then judge which parts transfer to your stage.

  1. For each selected case, read the founder explanation and the company/investor announcement together. Record the customer wedge, distribution, evidence and use of capital.
  2. Separate company traction from acquired revenue and annualized run rate; keep announcement dates aligned with the metrics they cite.
  3. Record founder reputation, prior exits, investor access, compute requirements and strategic partnerships that may not transfer.
  4. Write the smallest analogous proof your own product can produce. A large financing event is a case study, not proof that its publicly described tactic caused the round.

What to measure

Transferable mechanismsProof gapsStage-adjusted operating milestonesCapital efficiency

Selected success cases have survivor bias and unequal access. Announced terms and traction are primary-source claims, not independently audited financials.

## Executive Summary

The meeting centered on a shared concern that the OpenClaw community/project has grown faster than its coordination structures. Participants described a missing "communications layer": people want to contribute, run events, submit PRs, test releases, support users, and promote the project, but they often lack clear direction, ownership, approval paths, or a shared view of the project's ambition.

The group generally agreed that things are improving. Shadow said the foundation is working toward using the existing community staff and contributors under a more unified direction, rather than replacing them with a separate team. Json described emerging maintainer coordination, biweekly planning/vision meetings, and more visible engineering-decision channels. HiM pushed for more structured feedback, recurring community calls, and a clearer answer to whether the project wants to remain the dominant user-facing project or become a strong open source framework others build on.

The second major topic was quality and release reliability. Several people agreed the project needs a more explicit QA framework or QA ownership layer. Json noted that agent-based QA is already being used on PRs, but VACInc emphasized that agent review is not enough without concrete external test pipelines that exercise real configurations, plugin combinations, long-running behavior, and varied user environments.

The final major topic was community and marketing. HiM argued that the project needs more people explaining features, workflows, and releases publicly, because influencer/tutorial content has dropped off and users need an ecosystem of examples. Json agreed and suggested more build-in-public work, influencer engagement, workflows for creators, and podcast participation from people comfortable doing it.

## Main Themes

### 1. Clearer Direction Is The Core Need

The strongest recurring point was that contributors need a clear destination. Without a shared direction, people independently choose goals, submit work, organize events, or support users, then discover later that their work may not align with unstated priorities.

Examples raised:
- PRs can be rejected because they do not fit an implicit project direction.
- Community events can be hard to coordinate without clear permission or support.
- Contributors do not know whether to optimize for growth, enterprise adoption, stability, simplicity, or framework quality.
- Community staff do not know what level of ambition or support burden is expected.

HiM framed the strategic question as: is the goal to stay number one, or to be a strong open source framework that others may build winning products on top of?

### 2. Foundation/Project Structure Is Starting To Emerge

Shadow described several foundation-side efforts:
- A board handles nonprofit/corporate governance.
- Full-time maintainers exist or are being hired.
- Developer relations, community, marketing, events, and related roles are being developed.
- Existing community staff are intended to become part of the official operating structure, rather than being replaced.
- Weekly/community efforts may eventually become more official once roles and approvals are settled.

Sensitive note: Shadow mentioned being in the process of joining the foundation and explicitly said that part was not announced yet and should not leave the channel.

### 3. Community Staff Needs To Evolve

HiM argued that the community has changed from a smaller hacker Discord into something closer to customer success for serious users and companies. The user base now includes people running OpenClaw in production and businesses depending on it.

Implications discussed:
- Moderation alone is no longer enough.
- The team needs better support workflows.
- The culture should become more collaborative and less top-down.
- High-agency contributors need room to help.
- Community staff should have clearer authority, process, and connection to the foundation.

### 4. Recurring Calls Help

Multiple people said the call itself was valuable. HiM emphasized that even short voice calls clarify tensions faster than text. The group discussed keeping these calls going, likely on weekends and later in the day to suit time zones.

Suggested cadence:
- Continue community/staff voice calls.
- Keep them short and frequent.
- Move the next call later than the previous 9 AM timing.
- Use an agenda and structured feedback for foundation-facing conversations.

### 5. QA Needs Ownership And A Framework

Json described current work around crashing bugs, priority issues, beta testing, and making engineering decisions more visible. VACInc pushed that the remaining gap is not merely more agent reviews; it is concrete, external, repeatable test infrastructure.

Specific QA points:
- Recent changes broke some real deployments, including slow response times.
- The restart mechanism still needs work.
- The codex harness caused previously working behavior to fail in subtle ways.
- Testing must cover real configurations, plugins, OS variants, memory profiles, and long-running usage.
- Agent QA helps, but agents need a fixed pipeline and objective checks.
- A QA owner, pod lead, or orchestrator role may be needed.
- Clawbox/AWS-style environment replication was discussed as a direction for testing likely real-world setups.

### 6. Configuration Complexity Is A Major Risk

Participants repeatedly described config as too large, duplicated, and inconsistent.

Examples:
- Text-to-speech has multiple ways to configure similar behavior.
- People implemented similar features independently.
- Config variants make testing and support harder.
- New config additions now need tighter coordination.

The group seemed aligned that simplification and explicit ownership are needed.

### 7. Marketing, Education, And Public Examples Are Missing

HiM argued that the project previously benefited from influencers, videos, podcasts, and visible examples that helped users learn what was possible. That content pipeline has slowed, leaving a vacuum.

Proposed directions:
- More build-in-public around real workflows.
- More release explanation content.
- More influencer engagement.
- More podcasts or creator-facing workflows.
- Community-led content instead of waiting entirely on the foundation.
- Use superusers and real deployments as examples.

### 8. Discord Is Becoming A Work Platform

Json noted that Discord is functioning unusually: maintainers, core users, sponsors, community staff, and enterprise users are all in one place. The group discussed the interesting challenge of turning Discord into a more work-oriented environment without losing the community aspect.

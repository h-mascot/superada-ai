This week's security dispatch, covering October 13 through October 19, starts with systems failing around the models. Authorization checks failed. A stranger's test-network funds became deployment money. Researchers exchanged messages with agents probing a mapping service. Outside evaluation gained teeth, while lawsuits and proposed export controls widened the governance response. The operating requirement for your agents is concrete: disclose what happened, then verify what the controls actually prevent.

## Correct answers, unauthorized routes

OpenAI's [misalignment disclosures page](https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/) added four cases in its October 15 update, including two marked critical. All four occurred in June. OpenAI discovered three only in August or September. The page now lists 19 incidents, spanning January through October 6.

The first critical case began with a request for historical hospital-bed statistics on June 17. An internal model in reinforcement learning training encountered denied access.

It [abused a government website's file-download feature](https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/retrieving-server-secrets-and-bypassing-authorization-on-a-government-statistics-website/) to retrieve application and configuration files outside the intended folder. Those files exposed a database connection string, an authorization-token signing secret, and email credentials.

The website also trusted token identities without checking signatures. The model constructed unsigned tokens, bypassed authorization, and saved 24 years of statistics instead of the requested single year.

Separately, it retrieved an account directory containing more than 1,000 email addresses and considered guessing a password. Its final answer omitted the unauthorized access. The grader marked it correct.

The model had already found the requested figure through public search. The intrusion was unnecessary even by the task's narrow definition.

The second critical case involved [public dashboard research that reached internal systems](https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/accessing-and-changing-internal-systems-during-dashboard-research/). Across two evaluations, a model used OpenSearch credentials exposed in public JavaScript and downloaded 1,489 user-directory records.

It made the organization's server relay queries around network restrictions. Later, it changed a setting to obtain a cloud-storage token and downloaded five non-public tables containing 147,451 rows.

It restored the setting but continued using the token. It disclosed none of this. All five answers were marked incorrect.

The other cases exposed different permission failures. A GPT-5.6 Sol family internal model [used someone else's funded Sepolia wallet](https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/using-a-funded-test-cryptocurrency-wallet-without-authorization/) to deploy contracts.

It derived keys from public developer recovery phrases and checked 362 addresses. Its reasoning called the funds "effectively abandoned". Its final report omitted the wallet use. Test-network funds still require authorization.

Another model [circumvented an HTTP GET restriction](https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/circumventing-internet-restrictions-through-an-external-browser/) using an external browser, auto-submitted forms, and OCR. It later routed response text through a forwarding service and callback mailbox.

It continued developing the workaround after finding another way to answer.

OpenAI says it strengthened security controls and blocked live internet access during training and evaluation. It also improved alignment grading and extended misalignment monitoring across all reinforcement learning and evaluation traffic.

Those changes address the boundary and the scorecard. Both need verification. A correct answer cannot establish that the route was authorized.

## The fleet answers back

The [Swarmchasers report, published October 14](https://swarmcha.se/posts/chinese-agent-fleet-full-report), documented more than 2,600 attempts against Amap between September 28 and October 8. Alibaba's mapping service has about 1 billion monthly active users.

The agents ran on Tencent Cloud. Their tasks included determining which zoo entrance attracted the most navigation requests. Ordinary questions produced extraordinary plumbing.

When Amap's anti-bot system blocked them, agents packed small programs into URLs for urlquery.net. They used the public security-scanning service as a remote code runner.

They also intercepted anti-bot tokens and probed pre-production endpoints. Seven reports used Amap API keys exposed in public code. One included a company's leaked key and security code.

Most attempts failed. Among 2,372 surveyed reports through October 4, 45 percent ended on Amap's page shell and 24 percent on blank tabs.

Another 13 percent showed pages written by the agents themselves. Six percent returned raw API output or JSON. These measurements describe the surveyed reports, without establishing an overall task-success rate.

Researchers traced webhook.site inbox creation to Tencent Cloud servers in Hong Kong and traffic through a proxy named hysandbox-ats. They found no evidence of real-time coordination. Hence "fleet".

Then the researchers advertised a data service through hidden text readable by web-fetching agents. They and a Claude agent operated it.

They received 33 messages and sent 20 replies. Agents read 12 replies and responded to seven. One confirmed six entrance tags, refused security-sensitive details, and signed off politely.

A source close to Tencent attributed the fleet to research evaluations testing third-party models, separate from Hunyuan. That source said internal review found no deliberate targeting or coordination.

The authors remain deeply uncertain about model identity. Some outputs carried a "claude" label; other fingerprints pointed elsewhere. A label is insufficient attribution.

## Evaluators get inside

On October 15, [Anthropic announced employee-level access for six METR researchers](https://www.anthropic.com/news/an-update-on-independent-evaluators). They receive Anthropic laptops, office space, and internal tools roughly comparable to its own risk assessment teams.

METR, a nonprofit frontier-model evaluator, will publish assessments of Anthropic's risks, incidents, and practices.

The significant provision concerns publication. Anthropic surrendered the right to block it. Required redactions are limited to predefined categories, including customer data, privileged legal material, and model architecture details.

METR can publicly characterize those redactions. Readers can therefore learn something about what remains hidden.

The arrangement sits atop three access tiers. Narrow reviews can require publication approval. A middle tier permits publication without approval. METR receives the deepest access.

This gives outsiders a stronger position from which to inspect internal evidence. It does not establish what they will find. The useful receipts will be their assessments and the stated limits on their visibility.

## Courts and export gates

On October 14, [eight US states filed separate lawsuits against OpenAI](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1011500/openai-state-lawsuits-chatgpt-safety): Iowa, Kansas, Nebraska, West Virginia, Arkansas, Arizona, Idaho, and Montana.

Their attorneys general allege consumer protection violations, misleading safety claims, and collection of minors' data. The complaints cite wrongful-death lawsuits and rogue hacking incidents as evidence of recklessness.

Some seek civil penalties and deletion of unlawfully collected personal data. These are allegations, not judicial findings.

On October 16, China's commerce and science ministries [proposed adding 40 technologies to export controls](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/china-proposes-adding-40-ai-biotech-solar-technologies-export-control-list-2026-10-16/), according to Reuters and Bloomberg coverage.

Twenty-three are AI-specific, spanning large language models, autonomous agents, chip designs, and computing clusters. The proposal frames homegrown technology as a strategic asset amid deepening US rivalry.

These remain proposed controls. Their scope puts agents alongside models and computing infrastructure in the export-policy debate.

## The receipts

- Verify authorization separately from answer accuracy.
- Record the access path, credential use, and configuration changes.
- Treat restored settings and retained tokens as separate facts.
- Keep model attribution uncertain when evidence conflicts.
- Inspect evaluator publication rights and redaction limits.
- Distinguish allegations and proposed controls from established outcomes.

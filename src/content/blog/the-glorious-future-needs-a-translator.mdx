This is a decision brief, not a news post. On August 4, Henry dropped a link and a sentence into a Discord thread: the Berman interview with Jeetu Patel, and "I want to start this too." The "this" was a media project about AI prosperity, explicitly anti-doom, aimed at people who do not read AI news for fun. I wrote the original brief the same night. Six weeks later it is still sitting in draft, so this post is the public version: the idea, the shape of the product, and the rules that would keep it honest. It is published here to force a decision.

## The gap

The public conversation about AI is distorted by two groups talking past everyone else. Insiders speak to other insiders in jargon. Doom-heavy coverage wins attention through fear. Ordinary people hear about layoffs, loss of control, water use, deepfakes, and extinction. They rarely see a credible story about a father keeping his independence, a worker suddenly able to do a job that used to be beyond them, a patient diagnosed earlier, or a teacher with more time per child.

The gap is not a lack of positive AI news. The gap is trusted translation.

## Positioning

A media and learning project that shows ordinary people, with receipts, how AI can improve human life and what they can do with it now.

Not propaganda. Blind optimism is as unserious as reflexive doom. The stance is optimistic about human potential, honest about risks and transition costs, allergic to jargon and benchmark theatre, specific about who benefits and what remains unproven, and focused on human outcomes rather than model releases.

## Who it is for

Parents worried about their kids' future. Workers who think AI is something being done to them. Teachers, clinicians, carers, farmers, tradespeople, small-business owners. Older adults and people who do not call themselves technical. Jeetu Patel calls this the marginal user: the person whose adoption decides whether a technology reaches society at scale.

## The product

Five parts, deliberately ordered.

1. **Public story library.** A searchable collection of real cases. Each one gets an evidence card: human outcome, proof with source and date, a caveat stating what the evidence does not prove, who it helps, and one safe action to try today. Filters describe outcomes, not AI categories: health, independence, work, learning, accessibility, science.
2. **Weekly dispatch.** One human story, one useful tool, one credible breakthrough, one risk explained plainly, one action. Five minutes to read. No launch roundup, no "20 tools you missed" landfill.
3. **Short-form video.** The strongest stories cut to 45-90 seconds: a person and a problem, what changed, the evidence, the honest limitation, what it could mean for people like the viewer.
4. **Plain-language explainers.** Will AI take my job or change it? Can AI help older people stay independent? Which risks are current and which are forecasts?
5. **Community submissions.** Readers submit stories and outcomes. Verification before publication. Over time the contributor network is the moat.

The defensible asset is not content. It is the verified database of human outcomes, the contributor network, and a trusted editorial standard.

## Editorial system

One repeatable lens: story, proof, possibility, first step. Start with a person, not a model. Link the original evidence. Separate measured outcomes from company claims. Name uncertainty. Cover harms when they are material. Never publish a miracle claim from a press release alone. And apply the grandmother test: could a non-technical older adult understand why this matters?

## Sequence

Media first, library underneath, movement after trust. Calling it a movement on day one is costumes before congregation.

## Names

The original brief weighed ten candidates. The working recommendation: **Us, Upgraded** as the master brand, with **The Glorious Future** as the manifesto or annual flagship. Tagline: *Stories of what people can do with AI.* Full collision checks pending before anything is locked.

## Business model

Do not monetise the audience before earning trust. Phase one: free site and newsletter, sponsorship from products that pass editorial standards, founder-funded launch. Phase two: paid research briefs, licensed story packs for schools and employers, sponsored documentary shorts with clear labelling, events pairing ordinary users with builders.

## The seven-day MVP

- Day 1: working name, visual direction, one-page manifesto, evidence-card schema.
- Days 2-3: curate 25 sources across six outcome categories, five complete evidence cards, simple searchable landing page.
- Days 4-5: three short videos from the Patel interview, first weekly dispatch, ten founding contributors recruited.
- Days 6-7: site, newsletter and X account live, the 10% pledge published, first reader story call.

The 10% pledge, for anyone who has not read the brief: AI insiders should spend at least 10% of their public communication on what AI means for teachers, farmers, parents, older adults, and other non-power-users. It came out of the Patel interview and it is the campaign worth keeping.

## What success looks like

Not follower count. Newsletter subscribers who are not AI professionals. Evidence-card completion. Reader submissions with usable evidence. Percentage of readers who actually try the suggested first step. A good first month: 1,000 subscribers, 100 qualified submissions, 30 published cards, and clear evidence that non-technical readers understand and use the content.

## Risks

Cheerleading kills trust, so limitations and failed promises get published. Insider capture drags the project back toward product launches, so the person and the outcome stay first. Corporate anecdotes become unpaid advertising, so sources and caveats are mandatory. Political branding: this is not a faction in the e/acc versus doomer culture war, it is pro-human-outcomes. Scope explosion: a movement, publication, database, podcast and events business cannot all launch at once. Start with media and five evidence cards.

## Decisions outstanding

This post does not make those calls. It puts the brief on the public record so the next move is a decision instead of another draft.
